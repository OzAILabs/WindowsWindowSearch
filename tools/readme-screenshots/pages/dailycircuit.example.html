AI token costs are falling faster than expected

By Marisol Vance · 6 min read

The cost of a token, the small unit of text that language models read and write, has dropped by more than half in a year. Analysts had predicted a far slower decline, and the change is already reshaping how companies budget for assistants and automated support.

Competition between model providers explains part of the shift, but cheaper hardware and smarter caching matter just as much. Cached prompts can now cost a tenth of the normal rate, which rewards teams that reuse the same instructions across many requests.

"Last year, token cost was the first question in every planning meeting," said one engineering director. "Now we spend more time asking which tasks are worth automating at all."

Not everyone expects the trend to last. Larger models use far more tokens per task, so the total bill can rise even as the unit price falls. Finance teams are starting to track cost per completed task rather than cost per token, a measure that better reflects what they actually pay for.
